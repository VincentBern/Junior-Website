What's inside

The frameworks are public.
The wiring isn't.

Your Claude already knows what an ICP is. It'll write you one in thirty seconds. What it can't do is tell you that the ICP you just wrote contradicts the pricing you set three weeks ago — or that your go-to-market isn't answerable yet, because your value proposition was never validated.

That's what Junior adds. Not the frameworks. The network underneath them.

How a question travels

Your file stays on your disk. Our method stays on our server.

  1. You ask, from the tool you already have open.
  2. Junior works out which parts of your business the question actually touches.
  3. It reads only those, from your file, on your machine.
  4. It reasons over them with the method.
  5. You get a deliverable — not a template, not a lecture.

The method never comes down to your machine. Your Claude connects to it, asks it questions, and receives finished deliverables — never the frameworks themselves. Your answers travel the other way only in the moment they're needed, and are never stored on our side. The deliverable is the only thing that crosses.

The rhythm

An advisor doesn't answer once a quarter.

When you're building, decisions don't wait for the next session. Junior sits in the tool you already have open — you ask at 11pm on a Tuesday and you get the same methodology you'd get in a workshop. Every founder, every rep, every advisor asks from their own environment and gets the same answer, because it's the same framework underneath.

You work with Junior. You never work inside Junior.

The metaphor

He works on your plans. He has no claim on them.

An architect works on the building for as long as you keep calling. He just doesn't own it. No stake in what you put up, no cut of what it earns, and no hostage-taking of the drawings.

You call him when a decision needs a plan. You stop calling when it doesn't. Either way the plans are on your desk — and they still open without him.
